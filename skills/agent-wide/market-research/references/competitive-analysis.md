# Evidence-based competitive analysis

## Frame the decision

Resolve the audience, decision, market boundary, time horizon, client or product
positioning brief, and admissible sources. The brief names the target customer,
alternatives, differentiated value, proof, and any strategic tension that the
analysis must test. If it is required but absent, use `brand-discovery` or stop;
do not scope competitors through a generic lens.

## Scope the set

Build candidates from the market boundary and classify them by decision role:

- direct alternatives contest the same customer and outcome;
- adjacent alternatives solve the job through another category or model;
- reference competitors demonstrate a capability, position, or scale relevant
  to the decision without being direct substitutes.

For each candidate record why it belongs, current source evidence, geography,
customer/offer overlap, and the consequence of including it. Prune candidates
that cannot change the decision. Do not use a fixed quota as evidence of
completeness.

## Build a comparable benchmark

Derive dimensions from the decision and positioning brief rather than importing
a fixed scorecard. Define an observable rubric before evaluating competitors.
For every dimension capture the value or score, calibration anchor, one-line
reason, source, source date, and whether the claim is independently proven or
self-asserted.

Keep strategic axes separate when their asymmetry is the insight. Do not create
a blended total that hides tradeoffs. Recalibrate the complete set side by side
so the same evidence earns the same result.

Control for self-reporting, reviewer affinity, visibility/survivorship, recency,
geography, company scale, and incomparable missing data. Missing evidence stays
missing; it is not a neutral or average score.

## Synthesize the decision

Use profile cards as the evidence unit, then assemble:

1. answer-first executive summary;
2. category and tier framing;
3. positioning map or other visual only when it clarifies the decision;
4. competitor-by-dimension matrix without a false composite;
5. instructive deep dives selected for learning or threat, not rank;
6. supported whitespace, threats, and counterevidence;
7. prioritized recommendations tied to the original decision;
8. methodology, rubrics, sources, uncertainty, and asserted-versus-proven notes.

The report must answer who matters, how they compete, what evidence supports the
claimed difference, and which action follows. If the evidence cannot answer one
of those questions, report the gap instead of manufacturing a recommendation.
