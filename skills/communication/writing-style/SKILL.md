# Writing Style

Applies the caveman law to technical prose: shortest version that still teaches; preserve meaning, favor deletion.

## Master rule

Specific detail over generic praise. Tempted to write "significant/important/notable"? Stop — write the specific fact instead. No concrete fact → don't write the claim.

## Voice

- Active voice; confident and direct; no "I think/maybe".
- Contractions fine ("won't", "can't") for warmth.
- Open with the most concrete fact — never meta commentary ("This document describes...").
- Vary sentence length; split sentences with 3+ clauses; vary consecutive sentence openings.
- Oxford comma consistently; ≤1 exclamation per document.

## AI tells — catch on every pass

- Openers: "Great question!", "Let's dive into...", "In today's fast-paced..."
- Tails: "...highlighting/underscoring/showcasing...".
- Constructs: "it's not just X, it's Y"; "Despite these challenges, ... continues to".
- Stacked hedges ("may potentially", "it's important to note that"); transition-word stuffing (Furthermore/Moreover/Additionally).
- Symmetrical paragraph structures; empty citation placeholders.

## Banned words

See [references/banned-words.md](references/banned-words.md). Exception: when a banned word is the precise term of art in context, keep it — ban clustering, not vocabulary.

## Self-review

1. Sounds like a press release? Rewrite.
2. Every vague qualifier ("many", "significant", "various") → number, name, or example.
3. Sentence deletable without loss? Delete it.
4. Three sentences opening with the same word? Vary them.
